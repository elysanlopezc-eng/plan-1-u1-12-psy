Motivational Hook & Brain Break (~5 Minutes)

Central Inquiry Question: "Why do people make completely different choices when facing the exact same situation?"

Quick Class Discussion (Marketing Perspective)

Imagine two consumers standing in front of the exact same smartphone with identical prices. One buys it instantly; the other walks away. Why?

  • Is it visible behavior (actions) or hidden mental processes (feelings/perceptions)?
  • How does common sense differ from a scientific explanation?

Warm-up Brainstorming Trigger

Click to reveal the primary driver behind decision-making in psychological studies:

Insight: Human decisions are driven by a combination of observable behaviors (habits, environment) and internal cognitive/mental processes (emotions, beliefs, memory). Scientific psychology seeks to measure both accurately rather than relying on intuition!

Direct Instruction & Individual Practice (~25 Minutes)

10 min Concept Definition | 15 min Core Distinctions (Behavior vs. Mental Processes & 4 Scientific Goals)

Core Concept 1

Behavior vs. Mental Processes

Behavior: Any observable, measurable action (e.g., clicking an ad, speaking, writing).

Mental Processes: Internal, subjective experiences inferred from behavior (e.g., thoughts, motivation, memory, emotions).

Core Concept 2

The 4 Goals of Psychology

1. Describe: What is happening?
2. Explain: Why is it happening?
3. Predict: When will it happen again?
4. Influence: How can it be changed?

Individual Exercise: Identify & Classify

Classify the following items independently in your notebook before revealing the verification answer key:

Case A: "A customer smiles and hands cash to the cashier."
Classification: Overt Behavior (Observable action).
Case B: "A consumer feels anxious about overspending while browsing an online store."
Classification: Mental Process (Internal emotional state/cognition).
Case C: "Using buyer history to show personalized recommendations that increase sales by 15%."
Goal of Psychology: Predict & Influence/Control behavior.

Collaborative Team Activity (~30 Minutes)

Classifying Statements: Observation vs. Personal Opinion vs. Evidence-Based Claims

Team Leader Guides discussion & ensures habit focus
Data Recorder Documents team matrix and evidence
Critical Analyst Challenges assumptions & checks logic
Spokesperson Presents findings in plenary

Team Task: "The Scientific Marketing Audit"

Work in teams of 4. Analyze the statements below. Classify each into Observation, Opinion, or Evidence-Based Claim. Write your matrix on your team chart.

Statement for Analysis Team Classification Scientific Justification
1. "Red buy buttons are always better because red is prettier than blue." Opinion Subjective preference with no empirical control group data.
2. "78 out of 100 participants clicked the red button within 3 seconds during A/B testing." Observation Directly measurable, empirical factual data recorded without inference.
3. "Visual contrast increases user attention, which statistically improves conversion rates by 12% across 5 studies." Evidence-Based Claim Connects empirical observation with a tested psychological explanation.
